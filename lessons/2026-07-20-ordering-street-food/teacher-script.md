# Teacher Script — Ordering Street Food (2026-07-20)

## Phase 1 — Warm-up (0:00)

Say: "สวัสดีครับ Wei-Ting! สวัสดีครับ Anna! สบายดีไหมครับ?"
→ expect: "สบายดีค่ะ/ครับ" (+ particle — prompt with a hand gesture if missing,
don't say it).

Quick recall: "Last time — how do you say *nice to meet you*?"
→ expect ยินดีที่ได้รู้จัก yin-dii thîi dâai rúu-jàk. If missed, model once,
move on (stays in the queue).

## Phase 2 — Goal + context (0:05)

Say: "By the end of this hour you can walk up to any street stall in Bangkok
and order dinner — dish, spice level, extras, price, pay. Wei-Ting, this is
your next trip sorted."

Context hook: share the stall-menu photo in chat. Ask: "What do you recognize?"
(Both will spot 打拋 gà-phrao on any Thai menu aimed at Chinese tourists.)

## Phase 3 — Presentation (0:10)

Handout §2, row by row — say each word 2×, they repeat 2×, THEN let them see
the romanization (ear before eye).

Tone anchors to say out loud:
- ข้าว khâao: "falling — exactly Mandarin 4th tone, like 罵. And it's LONG —
  khaaao, not khao." (Drill เข้า khâo vs ข้าว khâao once here — Wei-Ting.)
- หมู mǔu: "rising — like Mandarin 2nd tone 麻. Anna — make it really rise."
- เผ็ด phèt: "low and SHORT, and the -t just stops — Anna, do your Cantonese
  一 jat ending. Wei-Ting, no puff at the end: phèt̚, not phè-tə."
- อร่อย à-rɔ̀i: "both syllables low. ɔ is the *law* vowel — rounder than o."

Board/screen text: type the spice ladder in chat:
ไม่เผ็ด mâi phèt → เผ็ดนิดหน่อย phèt nít nɔ̀i → เผ็ดมาก phèt mâak

Patterns (Handout §3), plain-English framing:
- "ขอ khɔ̌ɔ = *may I have* — the magic word. ขอ + food + ครับ/ค่ะ and you're polite everywhere."
- "เอา ao = *I'll take* — more casual; ไม่เอา = no thanks."
- "ไม่ mâi in front of anything = not: ไม่เผ็ด not spicy."
- "เท่าไหร่ thâo-rài = how much — same word-order as Chinese 多少, question word stays at the end."

## Phase 4 — Controlled practice (0:25)

**Drill A — the rice/white/news triple (listen first!):**
Point at the footer legend FIRST: "Remember — â falls, ǎ rises, à stays low.
Not pinyin."
Say ข้าว / ขาว / ข่าว in random order ×8; students type 1/2/3 for which they
heard. Target ≥80%, then flip: they produce, you call out what you heard.

Expected wrong answers and the fix:
- khâao said with pinyin-à (low) → hand gesture: chop downward for falling.
- khǎao flattened (Anna) → trace a checkmark ✓ in the air while she says it.

**Drill B — substitution:**
Frame: ขอ ___ ครับ/ค่ะ. Cues (English or 中文): basil pork / fried rice with
shrimp / chicken + fried egg / plain water. Then chain: dish + spice level in
one sentence.

## Phase 5 — Guided practice (0:35)

Dialogue Handout §4:
1. Teacher = vendor (แม่ค้า), students alternate as customer.
2. Swap: students take the vendor lines too (vendor lines use คะ/ค่ะ — remind
   Wei-Ting vendors can be any gender; he keeps ครับ as customer).
3. Vary the slots: dish (ไก่/หมู/กุ้ง), spice level, extra (ไข่ดาว), drink.

Culture note to drop in: "At a real stall you can just point and say ขอ อันนี้
khɔ̌ɔ an-níi — *this one, please*. Pointing at food is totally fine in Thailand."

## Phase 6 — Production (0:47)

Scenario: "You two are ordering dinner together from this menu photo. One of
you orders for both — two dishes, one with fried egg, drinks, ask the total,
pay. Go."
Round 2 (if time): Anna plays vendor — she improvises well; give her the price
"หกสิบบาท" card.

While listening, silently note errors on: vowel length (khâo?), final -t,
rising tone, particle drops. Do NOT interrupt.

If it stalls, feed: "Ask if it's spicy — เผ็ดไหม?"

## Phase 7 — Wrap-up (0:55)

1. Delayed correction: two items max, model → they repeat 2×.
2. Recap: point at the spice ladder; each student orders one imaginary dish
   perfectly as the "exit ticket".
3. Homework (Handout §7): voice-message order + 5 menu words copied in Thai
   script. Due Wednesday.
4. Preview: "Next: numbers & bargaining — so you can argue about the price
   like a local. Anna, that's your market lesson."
5. End: "เก่งมากครับ! See you Saturday."
